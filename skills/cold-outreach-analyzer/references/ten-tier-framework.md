# Ten-Tier Engagement and Support Framework

## Purpose and use

The bands describe the strongest defensible evidence of observed individualized engagement/support in the supplied interaction. They are an ordered heuristic for sorting evidence, not a published or validated admissions instrument and not an estimate of admission probability. Context, authority, and recruitment mechanism can change how a signal functions. Adjacent bands can overlap. Record the evidence and uncertainty rather than pretending the tiers form a universal probability order.

Use U outside the ten bands for missing or unscorable evidence. A verified formal offer or rejection is a separate outcome, never a tier.

## Tier definitions

| Tier | Evidence required | Examples and boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Individualized, concrete commitment to supervise and/or support a specific funding path for the named applicant, with any institutional approval conditions stated or still visible. | “If the program admits you, I will supervise you and have the funded RA line for your start.” A funding conversation, “top of my list,” future possibility, or “if admitted we can see whether funding is available” is not T1. T1 describes PI-level intent/support, not admission authority. |
| T2 | Explicit individualized advocacy to the people who decide admission. | “I will recommend you to the committee” or “I have asked the admissions committee to admit you.” Encouragement, a request to list a faculty name, or saying the application will be reviewed is not advocacy. No supervision commitment is implied. |
| T3 | Individualized, substantive selection interview or conversation in which the applicant is personally assessed or considered. | Record invitation, scheduled, and completed as separate states. A generic webinar, group information session, or a short interview every applicant receives is not selective evidence and is not T3. An interview is not an offer. |
| T4 | Substantive assessment of the applicant's research or readiness. | Examples include probing a research contribution, critically evaluating a proposal, or asking for a research task that is actually assessed. Routine CV/proposal upload or all-applicant documents are not T4. A research question may be T4 or T5 if it is unclear whether it evaluates the applicant; state that uncertainty. |
| T5 | Individualized research-fit discussion plus a concrete research-related next step, without clear evaluation or advocacy. | A professor discusses a specific project match and sets a follow-up to review a research plan. An open-ended warm chat without a concrete next step is not enough. |
| T6 | Applicant-specific routing or personal review is explicitly promised, without advocacy. | “Send your application ID and I will personally review it before our shortlist.” Generic “put my name on your application,” public instructions, or “I'll take a look” without a clear personal review commitment is not T6. |
| T7 | Current-cycle recruitment is explicitly confirmed, paired with generic encouragement. | “I plan to recruit a student this cycle; please apply.” It is a capacity signal, not individualized endorsement. Pairing this with an all-applicant interview does not make it T3. |
| T8 | Noncommittal positive wording or generic encouragement without confirmed current-cycle capacity. | “Your background sounds interesting; I encourage you to apply,” “top of my list” without a concrete commitment, or a warm but non-specific template. Preserve positive tone in the evidence note; do not infer an offer or active funded slot. |
| T9 | Procedural-only response or explicit lack of individual endorsement, without a final decision on the wider program route. | “Admissions are committee-based; I cannot comment before review,” “I cannot recommend you to my lab,” or generic process details. Accurate procedural explanations are not rejection evidence. |
| T10 | Explicit rejection or unavailability for the specified supervisor, program, or cycle. | “I cannot take a student this fall,” “I have no funding/space to supervise you this cycle,” or a formal program rejection. A PI's unavailability constrains that PI and cycle; it does not reject every lab or centralized program route unless the official decision says more. Keep reason codes: no capacity, no funding, fit, program rejection, or other. |

## U and formal outcomes

Use U (unscorable) for silence, missing reply text, an unclear speaker, or evidence that cannot be placed. Silence is missing evidence, not rejection; never force it into T10.

Record institutional decisions separately:

- **Formal offer:** identify whether an institution's official portal/letter is supplied. Do not infer a supervisor assignment, funding, or final enrollment from admission alone.
- **Formal rejection:** record the institution/program and date separately from a professor's route-specific unavailability.
- **Verbal report:** label it as reported but not independently established unless an official source is present.

## Independent dimensions to report

Do not collapse these into one score:

1. **Admission mechanism:** centralized committee; committee with supervisor matching; supervisor-led individual doctorate; advertised job; hybrid; unknown.
2. **Decision authority:** who can admit, shortlist, nominate, supervise, or allocate funds. A professor's influence is not automatically authority.
3. **Recruitment:** current cycle confirmed / not confirmed / no capacity / unknown; name whose capacity and the cycle.
4. **Funding:** documented / discussed / conditional / unavailable / unknown; identify PI grant, studentship, program package, external award, and approval conditions where known.
5. **Interaction state:** reply, scheduled interview, completed interview, requested/assessed work, advocacy, decision, or no reply.
6. **Outcome status:** none, pending, official offer/rejection, or user-reported outcome not verified.

## Confidence and mixed evidence

Tier confidence describes how clearly the supplied evidence meets a tier definition. It does not describe admission likelihood. A generic template can be classified confidently as T8; a phrase such as “I'll take a look” may be low confidence for T6 because its meaning is unclear.

Prefer separate dated signals over averaging. Later explicit cancellation of a position supersedes earlier advocacy for that same supervisor/cycle as the current route status; retain the earlier signal as history. If conflicting statements are unresolved, write “conflicting evidence” and list what must be clarified. If a single interaction plausibly falls into two adjacent bands, state the range and the missing discriminator.

The practical weight of evidence usually increases with an individualized, costly, or concrete action. But mechanism and authority still matter: a committee-based program can admit without a professor's advance contact, and a supervisor's enthusiasm can coexist with a later institutional rejection.

## Interpretation guardrails

- Do not infer that “top of my list,” “excited,” “looks like a fit,” or a funding discussion is a T1 commitment.
- Do not infer T2 advocacy from apply instructions, a named-faculty field, or a professor's willingness to review.
- Do not assume an interview is selective without knowing who received it, whether it is individual, and what it assesses.
- Do not treat a document requested from all applicants as an evaluation.
- A public “recruiting” notice establishes only the professor's stated current intent as of that page and access date; it is not an individualized endorsement or a guaranteed funded place.
- Committee language may accurately describe process. It does not imply rejection or lack of interest unless the message says so.
- No capacity / no funding is route-specific. Preserve alternative supervisors, program admission, later cycles, and externally funded options as unknown unless evidence closes them.
- Institution, department, degree, country, and year matter. Do not universalize a local rule.
- Never compute percentages or pseudo-probability scores from this rubric or from case anecdotes.

## Probability policy

Default output: “Acceptance likelihood: not estimable from this correspondence.” A numerical estimate would require a separate representative dataset matched to the relevant program, cycle, mechanism, and applicant/signal type, with denominators, censored outcomes, uncertainty, and validation. Such data is not supplied by individual anecdotes or this tier framework.
